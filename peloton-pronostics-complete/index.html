Learn the language / 06

What do the terms mean?

A quick guide to the jerseys, race terms and numbers in this dashboard.

Start here

One race. Four jersey competitions.

A jersey is a rider’s cycling shirt. Most show the rider’s team; four distinctive jerseys identify the leaders of separate Tour de France competitions. They are awarded during the race, and the final leaders win those classifications.

Data & methods

Edition coverage

2024: 141 final GC finishers, 124 listed points riders, 58 KOM records and 31 young riders. 2026: 158 final GC finishers, 121 listed points riders, 59 KOM records and 35 young riders. These editions use published final standings; individual stage results are not included, so daily charts, pivotal stages and the mountain-time proxy are available only for 2025.

2025: the supplied rider and result CSVs contain 184 riders, 3,602 stage records and 21 stages. The main time leaderboards use the 160 riders with all 21 stage results. Riders who exited are excluded from final time rankings.

Published versus calculated time

In 2025, adjusted time = recorded finish time − bonus + penalty. Calculated GC sums adjusted time over 21 stages. Daily GC uses riders with a complete record up to that day. Published standings can differ because of race-specific timing decisions. For example, the supplied records sum to 77h 02m 29s for the leader, while the published 2025 final GC total is 76h 00m 32s. Exact ties use published order for final tables and stable bib order for daily charts.

In 2024 and 2026, GC times, GC gaps and final ranks are taken from the published final GC dataset. The history panel always uses published ranks. Do not interpret differences in total race time between editions as a direct performance comparison: routes and timing rules differ.

Young riders and age

The 2025 young-rider view uses riders born on or after 1 January 2000 and ranks them by calculated GC. The 2024 and 2026 young-rider views use each edition’s published classification, independent of whether a birth date is available.

Age is calculated at the start of each edition: 29 June 2024, 5 July 2025 or 4 July 2026. Birth dates come only from the supplied rider file. Ages are unavailable for 59 of the 2024 finishers and 73 of the 2026 finishers; age filters exclude them unless “Age unavailable” is selected. “25 or under” is an age filter, not the official young-rider eligibility rule. Nationality is unspecified for 7 finishers in 2024 and 11 in 2026 where no matching source was collected.

Points, mountains and missing values

Points and KOM ranks are published final classifications from CyclingDB. The 2024 points list includes a −10 score, which is retained. A dash means no matching record was collected or the rider is not eligible; it is not assumed to be zero. Points and KOM totals remain final totals when inspecting an earlier 2025 stage.

Mountain time is an explicitly labelled proxy: cumulative adjusted time on the six 2025 stages labelled “mountain”, among the same 160 finishers. It is separate from the polka-dot classification and excludes the mountain time trial, labelled “individual time-trial”.

Using the page

Filters narrow the leaderboard while retaining full-field ranks. Jersey cards always summarize the whole edition. Selecting a rider opens their profile; checkboxes add up to three riders to a comparison. Switching editions preserves a selected rider where a final record exists and resets the filters. Team names and bib numbers belong to the selected edition. A 2025 pivotal day is the stage with the largest absolute change in calculated GC gap to that day’s leader.

The 2027 label describes the research goal, not a confirmed entry list or forecast. Historical performance alone does not provide betting probabilities. Pages 02–05 are described under “Pages 02–05” below.

Pages 02–05

Pages 02–04 and most of page 05 use the 2025 stage records, the only edition with a result for every rider on every stage. Stage terrain follows the provided stage types: flat, hilly, mountain and individual time trial. A top-10 is a stage finishing position of 10 or better. Positions are the order riders crossed the line; riders in the same group share a time but keep separate positions.

Stage winners: a bunch finish means at least 20 riders were given the winner’s time; a solo win means nobody else had that time; the margin is the gap to the first rider with a slower time (for time trials, the gap to second place). Route map: straight lines join each stage’s start and finish towns, so they show where the race went, not the roads raced.

Consistency is the average finishing position across all 21 stages and its standard deviation, for the 160 finishers. Exits: “DNS” means the rider did not start that stage; “DNF” means they withdrew during it (provided exits.csv).

Team classification adds each team’s three best raw finish times on every stage, without bonuses or penalties; ties go to the team with more stage-day wins. The calculated top 10 matches the published classification, times included. Who scored for the team shares out the team’s top-10 stage finishes by rider. Team registration countries come from the provided team file.

Countries: “per 100 starts” divides stage wins or top-10s by stage starts (one rider on one stage), so a 2-rider nation can be compared with a 38-rider nation; nations with fewer than 40 starts are flagged as small samples. Ages are measured on 5 July 2025. The country × jersey heatmap uses calculated 2025 overall and young-rider ranks (which match the published ones) and published points and mountains ranks; for 2024 and 2026 it uses the published final classifications, and some nationalities were not collected.

Every figure on pages 02–05 is checked against an independent pandas calculation from the provided CSVs (betting-dashboard/tests/jay_tabs.test.mjs).
